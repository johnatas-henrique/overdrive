## Track Identity

Monza is unique: it's the only circuit built inside a **royal park** (Parco di Monza, 688 hectares). In 1989, Monza was undergoing a major transformation — the brand-new **pit complex** was under construction (completed for the 1990 Italian GP). The previous pit building (1970s) was still in use for the 1989 race. The old high-speed oval banking (Sopraelevata) was disused but still standing — a ghostly concrete ruin visible from parts of the circuit. Trees line almost the entire lap. The atmosphere is "cathedral of speed" — ancient park, high speeds, echoes of history.

**Per-track trackside objects** listed below are in ADDITION to shared assets (ASSET-101 barriers, ASSET-103 grandstands, etc.).

## Key Visual References

- **Park setting:** Monza is inside a 688-hectare royal park — the only circuit with this setting
- **High-speed:** Temple of Speed — very long straights, flat track, minimal corners
- **Aged concrete:** Unlike the other tracks, Monza's structures have a sense of age and history
- **Contrast:** New 1989 pit building against the old park, old garages, old banking ruins
- **Sopraelevata:** The disused oval banking is a unique visual feature — no other circuit has a ruined track visible from the active circuit
