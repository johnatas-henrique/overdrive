## Track Identity

Silverstone in 1989 was an ex-RAF airfield: flat, open, windswept. The circuit had NO Woodcote chicane (removed 1987), making it a high-speed blast. The 1987 pits were a major upgrade: 40 concrete garages in a 2-story block with press center above. The circuit is surrounded by grass and gravel — no trees or buildings along most of the lap except the pit/start complex. The "Wing" complex didn't exist yet — that's a 2011 addition.

**Per-track trackside objects** listed below are in ADDITION to shared assets (ASSET-101 barriers, ASSET-103 grandstands, etc.).

## Track-specific Notes

Silverstone is the **most open** of the 4 MVP tracks. The majority of the lap has no buildings at all — just grass, gravel, and sky. This is by design (airfield). Trackside objects are concentrated in:

1. **Pit/Start area** — pit building, main grandstand, start gantry, billboards
2. **Key corners** — distance boards, flag posts, tire barriers
3. **Spectator areas** — small grandstands and grass banks at Copse, Becketts, Stowe, Club

**No unique buildings to model.** Trees are minimal (Silverstone is open farmland). This makes Silverstone the simplest to block out — mostly barriers, distance boards, and the pit complex.

## Key Visual References

- Open sky: Silverstone's defining feature — no buildings, no trees, no elevation
- Wide track: very wide compared to other circuits (former runways)
- Flat: the only flat circuit of the 4 (Monaco = hilly, Spa = very hilly, Monza = flat)
- Green/brown palette: grass + concrete + white gravel
- Spectator mounds: grass banks for spectators at some corners (no grandstand needed)
