Overdrive Asset Production · Lesson 1

Style, Technique, Standard — and how to prove an asset is "on style"

2026-08-06 · Mission: MISSION.md · ~20 min

The problem: "I like it" is not conformance

You generated 93 images for the McLaren (team_tier1_a) with the prompts from the asset spec. You liked them. We then checked 6 of them against the art bible with an objective checklist. The result: 2 conform nearly, 2 conform partially, and 2 failed outright — one with the wrong livery color, one with a completely different car (green/yellow/blue instead of white/red).

That is the core lesson of this course: liking an asset is a feeling; conformance is a measurement. Overdrive needs 16 cars and 16 tracks that look like one game. Feelings don't scale to 16 cars — a checklist does.

The hierarchy: Style → Technique → Standard

Three layers that people confuse. They answer three different questions.

LayerQuestionOverdrive exampleWhere it lives
Style (identity)What does the game feel like?"Hand-crafted 1990s Japanese racing anime": Fujishima's heroic clarity + Hino Matsuri's delicate lineworkArt Bible §1
Technique (how we render it)How do we implement the style in the engine?Painted textures (cel-style), URP Simple Lit with controlled specular, Unlit for sky/grass. "No PBR metallic."Art Bible §8.5
Standard (numbers)What are the budgets?LOD0 35k tris, 2048² texture, ASTC 6×6, 8–12 texels/m, ≤150 draw callsArt Bible §8.1 / §8.3

"Texturas pintadas + Simple Lit" is not the style — it is the technical implementation the art bible chose to materialize the style in 3D. When someone says "NPR" they usually mean a family of techniques (cel shading, toon ramps, outlines, matcap) — Overdrive uses a specific subset of them.

Why copying the prompt does not guarantee the style

A prompt is an instruction in text, not a contract. What the model draws depends on things the prompt text does not control: the checkpoint/base model, the sampler, steps, CFG, seed, LoRAs, resolution. The same prompt with different seeds gives different images, and different models interpret "cel-shaded" differently.

Evidence from your own output folder:

  • team_tier1_a-2_00030_.png — cockpit of a gray/silver car. Livery was supposed to be white + fluorescent red.
  • team_tier1_a-2_00059_.png — cockpit of a green/yellow/blue car. Completely different car.
  • img_00172_.png … — the model wrote "Kosuke" on the car body. The spec says "no text".
  • Nearly every image has tire sidewall text (Pirelli, Bridgestone) — the model loves to write.

Conclusion: the prompts raised the probability of the style; they did not guarantee it. Production needs a verification step after every generation. That step is a checklist.

The conformance checklist (8 criteria)

Extracted from the art bible and the asset spec. These are the questions to ask of every generated image:

#CriterionWhat to look at
1Flat gray backgroundSolid #CCCCCC, no environment, no scenery
2No text, no real logosTires, cockpit, bodywork — geometric blocks only
3Flat cel shadingHard shadow edges, two-tone; no smooth photographic gradients
4No photo-realism artifactsNo carbon-fiber weave, no dirt/scratches, no lens reflections, no fine PBR detail
51989 F1 silhouetteLow nose, open cockpit, exposed wheels, high rear wing, no halo
6Livery colors correctAgainst the palette in Art Bible §2.2 (e.g. Marlboro Red #E03C31)
7Anime drawing styleClean linework, bold color shapes, heroic proportions
8Overall: 2D anime illustrationDoes it read as an illustration, not a 3D render or photo?

Score each item PASS / PARTIAL / FAIL. Any FAIL means the image needs a prompt fix and a regenerate — or an explicit accept decision.

How to apply it (your tools)

You don't need a trained eye. You need criteria + a tool that can see:

  1. The vision agent. This session's model cannot see images, but the project's vision subagent can. Delegate with the checklist in the prompt (copy the block below) and the file path(s). It returns a per-criterion table + verdict.
  2. Programmatic checks (next lesson): extract dominant colors with a script to verify background #CCCCCC and palette conformance objectively.
You are performing a visual style-conformance check for the game Overdrive
(1990s Japanese racing anime style). Look at the image(s) and score each one
against this checklist, item by item, PASS/PARTIAL/FAIL:
1. Flat gray background (no environment)  2. No text, no real logos
3. Flat cel shading (hard shadow edges)   4. No photo-realism artifacts
5. 1989 F1 silhouette (low nose, open cockpit, exposed wheels)
6. Livery colors correct (white body + fluorescent red sidepods)
7. Anime drawing style (clean, bold shapes)  8. Overall: 2D anime illustration
Then give a one-line verdict per image: CONFORMS / NEARLY CONFORMS / DOES NOT CONFORM.

The 4 systematic problems and their prompt fixes

ProblemPrompt fix
Text/logos on tires and cockpitAdd: "no text on tires, no text anywhere, pure geometric shapes only"
Carbon-fiber / material textureAdd: "flat painted surfaces, no material textures, no carbon fiber"
Smooth gradients instead of celAdd: "two-tone shading, hard shadow edges, no smooth gradients"
Livery not pinned in cockpit promptsPaste the FULL livery description into every prompt (the cockpit prompts only said "red and white livery panels" — the model filled in the rest)

Expected answers

Reference A (front): PASS on 1 (gray background), 5 (period silhouette), and 6 (white/red livery). PARTIAL on 3, 7, and 8 because the rendering still has smooth gradients and mechanical detail. FAIL on 2 if tire lettering or invented marks are legible. Verdict: NEARLY CONFORMS.

Reference B (side): PASS on 1 (gray background), 5 (low nose, open cockpit, exposed wheels), and 6 (white/red livery). PARTIAL on 3, 7, and 8 because the painted shadows are softer than strict cel shading. FAIL on 2 if the tire lettering is readable. Verdict: NEARLY CONFORMS.

Both are usable as study references because their remaining issues are named and fixable at the prompt or paint stage.

Quick recall question: the model wrote "Kosuke" on a car body in img_00172_. Which checklist criterion fails, and what does the fix look like?

Answer
