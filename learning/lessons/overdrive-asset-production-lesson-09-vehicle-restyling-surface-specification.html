Overdrive Asset Production · Lesson 9

Vehicle restyling: from art bible to surface specification

2026-08-09 · Mission: MISSION.md · ~60 min

The question this lesson answers

How do you turn a visual direction into a concrete vehicle surface specification without remodeling the vehicle first?

A vehicle becomes stylistically coherent when its mesh, livery, material response, shader, palette, and presentation obey the same contract.

Lesson 8 isolated one material change on a small prop. This lesson scales the same discipline to a vehicle, where the surface is divided into several material domains and where a shader experiment can easily be mistaken for a complete art direction.

1. The art bible is the surface contract

The Overdrive art bible already defines the approved baseline. A new shader may be tested, but it does not become the production standard merely because it looks more “anime.” The result must still satisfy the established visual language.

Vehicle domainApproved baselineWhat to avoid
Car paintURP Simple Lit with controlled specular responsePBR metallic workflow or uncontrolled reflections
CarbonURP Simple Lit, matte and low specularPhotoreal carbon weave and excessive gloss
CockpitURP Simple Lit with a flat painted responseUncontrolled dark realism that hides the interior
TireURP Simple Lit, near-black and low specularBright rubber highlights or decorative noise
HelmetURP Simple Lit with a controlled glossy shellDifferent lighting logic from the car
Grass, sky, and selected flat surfacesUnlit painted colorsForcing every surface into one shader family

The art bible also prohibits normal maps on painted surfaces, specular maps, and PBR metallic workflow. These are not optional style preferences; they are constraints on the surface specification.

2. Divide the vehicle into surface domains

Do not describe a vehicle as having one material called “the car.” A reusable surface specification names the domains that need different visual treatment:

Vehicle
├── CarPaint
├── Carbon
├── Cockpit
├── Tire
├── Helmet
├── Mirror
└── Exhaust

The hierarchy may not use these exact names. The task is to map the imported renderer slots and material names to semantic roles before changing anything.

QuestionEvidence
Which object owns the visible body?MeshRenderer and material slot inspection
Which materials are shared?Project material reference and instance comparison
Which textures are base color maps?Material shader properties and texture references
Which parts must remain independent?Domain role: tire, cockpit, glass, carbon, or body

3. Write the surface specification before editing

A surface specification is a decision record for one vehicle. It should be specific enough that another person can reproduce the result without interpreting the art bible from scratch.

Vehicle: [vehicle identifier]
Reference role: [hero car / rival / civilian / learning asset]

Geometry:
  Mesh change: none for this pass
  Silhouette decision: preserve historical anchor

Surface domains:
  CarPaint: [material route, palette, gloss target]
  Carbon: [material route, matte target]
  Cockpit: [readability target]
  Tire: [value and specular target]
  Helmet: [color and gloss target]

Texture:
  Base-map source: [file]
  Painted information: [what is drawn]
  Unwanted detail: [what must be removed]

Shader experiment:
  Baseline: URP Simple Lit
  Candidate: [Unlit / cel-ramp / edge outline]
  Reason: [one visual question]

Presentation:
  Camera: [fixed camera]
  Lighting: [fixed lighting state]
  Background: [fixed background]

The specification prevents a shader experiment from silently becoming a geometry change, a livery redesign, or a lighting comparison.

4. What an “anime shader” can and cannot solve

TechniqueCan improveCannot replace
Simple Lit tuningSpecular strength, matte response, readable volumeMissing livery design or wrong silhouette
UnlitStable painted colors and flat graphic presentationNatural volume, ground contact, and scene lighting response
Cel or ramp shadingDiscrete light and shadow bandsA coherent palette or painted texture
Depth/normal outlineVisible silhouette and 3D face-edge emphasisCorrect UV placement or livery graphics
Rim lightSeparation from the background and a graphic accentMaterial identity and historical color accuracy

The shader is one layer of the result. If the mesh is too realistic, the livery is wrong, the texture contains photographic detail, or the palette contradicts the team identity, a more anime-looking shader will not repair the full asset.

5. Approved, experimental, and rejected decisions

Every candidate should receive a status instead of being described vaguely as “cool” or “more anime.”

StatusMeaningEvidence required
Approved baselineMatches the art bible and is safe for production experimentsMaterial, palette, lighting, and performance checks
ExperimentalUseful for comparison but not yet a production ruleBefore/after capture and a stated visual question
RejectedCreates a contradiction, artifact, or unacceptable costConcrete failure evidence
BlockedCannot be judged because a required input is missingNamed missing asset, camera, texture, or measurement

6. The one-variable vehicle experiment

  1. Choose one vehicle instance and record its current renderer-to-material routes.
  2. Duplicate only the material or material set required for the experiment.
  3. Keep the mesh, UVs, hierarchy, camera, light, and background fixed.
  4. Capture the baseline before changing the shader or material property.
  5. Change exactly one variable: shader family, smoothness, specular response, base color, or texture.
  6. Capture the same view after the change.
  7. Compare silhouette, color masses, surface response, graphic clarity, cockpit readability, and ground contact.
  8. Classify the result as approved, experimental, rejected, or blocked.

For a shader comparison, the strongest first matrix is:

Vehicle_Surface_01_SimpleLit_Baseline
Vehicle_Surface_02_SimpleLit_LowSpecular
Vehicle_Surface_03_Unlit_Reference
Vehicle_Surface_04_CelRamp_Experimental
Vehicle_Surface_05_DepthNormalOutline_Experimental

Do not compare all five at once in the final decision. Use the matrix to identify the visual mechanism, then repeat the winning candidate against the fixed baseline.

7. Read the result like a production reviewer

CriterionPass conditionFailure signal
Historical silhouetteRecognizable vehicle proportions remain intactShader test hides or distorts the silhouette judgment
PaletteTeam colors read as intentional massesLighting changes the apparent team identity
TexturePainted information remains legible and controlledPhotoreal noise dominates the surface
Material responsePaint, carbon, tire, and cockpit separate clearlyEvery domain looks equally glossy or equally flat
Outline or edge treatmentLines reinforce readable structureLines appear on every tiny detail or through occluders
PresentationResult works under the fixed Overdrive lighting stateIt only works under a special test light

Controlled exercise

  1. Choose the Porsche CARRERA or another reusable vehicle learning asset.
  2. Record the current material domains and the base shader.
  3. Capture a fixed baseline with the car in the same camera and light setup.
  4. Create one isolated material route for the body paint.
  5. Run a Simple Lit low-specular comparison.
  6. Run an Unlit comparison only as a visual reference.
  7. If available, run a cel/ramp or depth/normal outline comparison as an experimental candidate.
  8. Do not change the mesh, UVs, livery texture, camera, or light during the shader comparison.
  9. Record which technique improves the surface and which problem remains unsolved.
Expected reasoning

A successful result is not “the car looks more anime.” It is: the visual question was isolated, the approved Simple Lit baseline remained available, the material route was independent, the same presentation was used, and the candidate was classified from visible evidence.

Verified exercise result

Decision rule

art bible
  → surface domains
  → baseline material route
  → one-variable shader experiment
  → identical presentation
  → visual and technical comparison
  → classify the candidate
  → record the production surface specification
