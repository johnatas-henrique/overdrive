Overdrive Asset Production · Lesson 4

Materials, shaders, and surface response

2026-08-06 · Mission: MISSION.md · ~35 min

The question this lesson answers

Lesson 3 taught you to locate a problem. This lesson explains what happens when the geometry is already acceptable but the surface looks wrong.

How do texture, material, shader, and light combine to make the same mesh look painted, metallic, matte, transparent, or damaged?

The practical goal is not to memorize Unity property names. It is to look at a surface and identify which part of the rendering chain is responsible for what you see.

1. The four layers of a visible surface

A surface seen in the scene is the result of several layers working together:

  1. Texture: image data such as color, decals, roughness, normal information, or emission.
  2. Material: the selected textures and numeric values assigned to a surface.
  3. Shader: the program that interprets the material and calculates the rendered result.
  4. Lighting: the scene light, environment, shadows, and reflections that reveal the surface response.

The same mesh can therefore look completely different without changing its geometry:

same mesh
  + painted texture + matte material + controlled light = graphic painted surface
  + metallic material + smooth surface + reflections = polished metal
  + transparent surface + glass settings + light transmission = glass
  + normal map + strong specular + hard light = realistic micro-detail

2. Texture is not the material

A texture is an image. A material decides how that image participates in rendering.

The Ferrari body gives us a concrete example:

Mesh:       fer_body-3
Material:   643_BODY / asr-car-body.mat
Base Map:   asr-car-body.dds
Shader:     Universal Render Pipeline/Lit

The base texture provides the red paint and historical livery. The material adds the surface response. If the material's smoothness is increased, the red paint reflects more strongly. If the shader or material receives a normal map, small-scale surface variation can appear even though the mesh itself has not changed.

This is why “the texture is correct” does not prove that the asset looks correct. The same texture can look matte, glossy, metallic, flat, or noisy depending on the material and shader.

4. The Ferrari damage-map example

The Ferrari learning import initially showed irregular dark marks across the red body. The body texture itself was not the cause. Unity inspection found:

asr-car-body.mat
  _BaseMap  → asr-car-body.dds
  _BumpMap  → damage.dds
  _NORMALMAP → enabled

The game has no damage presentation in this learning scene. The correction was therefore to remove damage.dds from the material's normal-map slot and disable the normal-map keyword in the learning copy. The body mesh and the main livery texture remained unchanged.

This is the pattern to remember:

  • If the shape is correct and the color image is correct, inspect the material inputs before rebuilding the mesh.
  • A texture can be valid as a source file and still be wrong in a particular material slot.
  • Removing an unwanted material input is different from deleting the texture asset.

5. URP/Lit, Simple Lit, and Unlit

These shaders are not interchangeable names for “quality.” They are different rendering strategies.

Shader familyWhat it is useful forOverdrive reading
URP/LitFull physically based lighting features and many surface controlsUseful for inspection and selected realistic surfaces, but easy to make too reflective or detailed
URP/Simple LitControlled lit response with a simpler surface modelPreferred direction for car paint, carbon, cockpit, and tires in the art bible
UnlitTexture/color is shown without normal scene-light responseUseful for flat painted surfaces, sky, grass, crowd, or other graphic elements

Overdrive does not use “no lighting” as a universal rule. It uses lighting selectively. The intended identity is painted textures plus controlled Simple Lit response, with Unlit reserved for surfaces that should remain graphically stable.

6. Reading the Porsche and Ferrari together

The Porsche's main material was inspected in Unity as Assets/Prototype/RaceFeel/Materials/CARRERA_4096.mat:

  • shader: Universal Render Pipeline/Lit;
  • smoothness: 0.483;
  • metallic: 0.102;
  • environment reflections: enabled;
  • shadow reception: enabled.

Those values help explain why the Porsche reads as a more realistic sports car. Its mesh is not the only reason. The material permits a stronger reflective response than the Overdrive art direction generally wants.

The Ferrari arrived with 33 external materials and 74 recognized textures. Its materials use Universal Render Pipeline/Lit after import. That makes the model visible and inspectable, but it does not make it final. A production pass would still need to consolidate material groups and convert them toward the Overdrive mapping.

7. Surface diagnosis exercise

For each observation, identify the most likely first place to inspect. Do not jump directly to remodeling.

ObservationFirst inspection target
The logo is stretched or mirroredUVs, decal texture, and material assignment
The body has the correct color but looks wetSmoothness, metallic, specular, and shader
Dark scratches appear across otherwise clean paintNormal map, detail map, damage map, or material keyword
The glass is opaqueSurface mode, blend mode, alpha, and shader
The mesh is correct but stays bright in shadowShader choice, lighting response, and whether Unlit was used
The car has the right material but the wrong silhouetteMesh and proportions, not shader

8. Controlled experiment

Use one isolated material or simple prop for the experiment. Do not change the Ferrari's tire lettering or side decal yet; those belong to the UV/decal lesson.

  1. Record the baseline screenshot and material properties.
  2. Change only one property, such as smoothness.
  3. Capture the same camera view under the same light.
  4. Describe exactly what changed: reflection sharpness, brightness, color, shadow readability, or transparency.
  5. Restore the baseline before changing a different property.

The purpose is to build a causal vocabulary:

“It looks too realistic”
  → “The geometry is acceptable, but smoothness and reflections are too strong.”

“The surface has strange dark marks”
  → “A damage texture is connected to the normal-map slot.”

“The logo is wrong”
  → “This is probably a UV, decal texture, or material assignment problem.”

Decision rule

Before modifying a mesh, ask:

  1. Is the silhouette correct?
  2. Is the texture itself correct?
  3. Is the texture assigned to the correct material slot?
  4. Are the material properties appropriate for the surface?
  5. Is the shader appropriate for the Overdrive visual goal?
  6. Is the lighting exposing a real problem or merely exaggerating a response?

If the answer to the first question is yes, do not remodel until the other layers have been checked.
