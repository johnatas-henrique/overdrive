Anatomy of a 3D asset: where does the problem live?

2026-08-06 · Mission: MISSION.md · ~30 min

The question this lesson answers

When an asset feels wrong, “it does not fit” is a useful alarm but not yet a diagnosis. This lesson gives you a sequence for locating the problem:

Does the problem live in the mesh, the silhouette, the proportions, the UVs, the texture, the material, the shader, the prefab, or the context?

We will compare two very different vehicles: the Porsche Carrera from the RaceFeel prototype and an ASR 1991 Formula car. The Porsche is inspected directly in Unity. The ASR source is outside the Unity project, so its technical facts come from the import playbook and its real FBX source path; it has not been imported or modified as part of this lesson.

1. Mesh and geometry

The mesh is the polygonal surface that gives an object its three-dimensional shape. Geometry is broader than polygon count. It includes:

  • volumes and curves;
  • topology: how polygons connect;
  • modeled details;
  • separate parts and hierarchy;
  • collision geometry;
  • LOD variants.

The Porsche prefab contains 44 objects, including body, doors, glass, wheels, brakes, mirrors, lamps, spoiler, steering wheel, collider, and a disabled CARRERA_LOW visual variant. That modularity is useful: different parts can receive different materials and can be animated or replaced independently.

The ASR pipeline uses a different kind of hierarchy. Its expected names include TYRE_*, RIM_*, WHEEL_*, SUSP1-4, STEER_HR, and ARROW_RPM. Those names are not cosmetic; downstream animation and import scripts depend on them.

2. Silhouette

The silhouette is the outer contour that remains if the object is filled completely black. It is one of the fastest ways to identify whether an asset belongs to the correct category.

AssetSilhouette communicatesPossible Overdrive role
Porsche CarreraClosed-roof civilian sports carCharacter car, paddock vehicle, cutscene prop
ASR Ferrari 6431991 open-wheel Formula carRace car, grid, garage, replay, hero shot

Texture cannot turn the Porsche into an F1 car. It can make the Porsche a better Overdrive civilian car. Conversely, the ASR silhouette already belongs to the racing category, even if its surface and geometry detail are too realistic for the final art direction.

3. Proportions

Proportion means the relationship between parts: wheel diameter to body height, cockpit size to car length, wing width to chassis width, and so on.

Anime styling does not require every proportion to become unrealistic. Overdrive should preserve important historical anchors in its F1 cars: low nose, open cockpit, exposed wheels, and period-appropriate wings. Stylization can come from simplification, clearer shapes, controlled exaggeration, and graphic surface treatment.

For the Porsche, real-world road-car proportions are acceptable if its role is a civilian vehicle. The question is not “does it look like a real Porsche?” The question is “does it look like the right type of Porsche for this role, and can its surface treatment belong to Overdrive?”

4. UVs

UVs are the 2D layout that tells a texture where to appear on a 3D mesh. Imagine cutting the surface of a cardboard object and unfolding it flat. The unfolded pattern is the UV layout.

Good geometry can still be difficult to repaint if:

  • UV islands are too small or fragmented;
  • left and right sides overlap when unique markings are needed;
  • the texture resolution is wasted on hidden surfaces;
  • decals stretch across seams.

The ASR playbook warns that material consolidation and texture routing are major import tasks. The Lotus test carried 43 materials, while the target is approximately 5–10 semantic groups: paint, carbon, interior, glass, rubber, brakes, and LEDs. That is a pipeline problem, not merely a color problem.

5. Texture and surface

A texture is an image applied to the UV layout. It can provide base color, painted shadows, decals, roughness information, normal information, or emission. Overdrive's art bible prioritizes painted textures and explicitly prohibits normal maps for the car body workflow.

The Porsche currently uses CARRERA_4096.png as its main base texture. Its original surface communicates a more realistic sports car through paint, glass, tire detail, and material separation. A restyle could preserve the mesh while changing the surface language:

  • replace realistic decals and logos;
  • reduce micro-detail;
  • paint clearer color blocks;
  • remove or reduce normal maps;
  • make highlights intentional instead of physically descriptive.

6. Material and shader

A material combines textures and numeric responses such as smoothness, metallic value, specular color, emission, and transparency. A shader is the program that uses those inputs to render the surface.

Unity inspection found that the Porsche's main material, Assets/Prototype/RaceFeel/Materials/CARRERA_4096.mat, uses Universal Render Pipeline/Lit, smoothness 0.483, metallic 0.102, environment reflections, and shadow reception. Its matte material uses the same shader with smoothness and metallic set to zero.

This gives us a concrete diagnosis: the Porsche's geometry is separate from the fact that its material currently permits a more realistic light response. Changing the shader/material can change its visual language without rebuilding the mesh.

The ASR playbook requires imported materials to be converted to URP Simple Lit with per-group smoothness values. It also warns about empty texture nodes, wrong normal-map assignments, accidental alpha links, and excessive material counts. These are surface-pipeline problems even when the car's silhouette is already useful.

7. Prefab and Unity role

A prefab is the reusable Unity package that combines an object's hierarchy, components, materials, colliders, and configuration. The same mesh can be useful or useless depending on how it is packaged.

8. Context and camera

Context is the asset's role, placement, neighbors, lighting, and camera. A Porsche viewed in a warm garage beside an Overdrive mechanic can feel correct as a character vehicle. The same Porsche placed on the starting grid beside 16 Formula cars would communicate the wrong gameplay role.

The camera does not normally move a fixed directional light. It changes which surfaces, shadows, and highlights are visible. A front view may show the painted hood and headlights; a rear view may reveal reflective glass, exhaust detail, and a different material balance. Camera and lighting are therefore part of the final presentation even when the asset itself is unchanged.

Decision vocabulary

After this comparison, use one of four decisions:

  • Use directly: geometry, surface, and role already fit.
  • Re-style: geometry and role fit; surface/materials do not.
  • Simplify or remodel: category is correct, but geometry detail or proportions fight the style.
  • Reference only: valuable for history or study, but not a good production base.

Exercise

Choose one hypothetical change and classify it:

  1. Replace the Porsche's realistic paint texture with flat Overdrive color blocks.
  2. Turn the Porsche into an open-wheel F1 car by painting red sidepods onto it.
  3. Use the ASR car's 1991 silhouette but consolidate its materials and remove normal-map detail.
  4. Place the Porsche in a character's paddock cutscene with Overdrive-painted materials.
Expected classification

1 is re-style. 2 requires remodeling; paint cannot change the road-car silhouette into an F1 silhouette. 3 is re-style plus pipeline simplification, assuming the geometry and proportions remain acceptable. 4 is contextual use after re-style.
