Anatomy of a 3D asset: where does the problem live?

2026-08-06 · Mission: MISSION.md · ~35 min

The question this lesson answers

When an asset feels wrong, “it does not fit” is a useful alarm but not yet a diagnosis. This lesson gives you a sequence for locating the problem:

Does the problem live in the mesh, the silhouette, the proportions, the UVs, the texture, the material, the shader, the prefab, or the context?

We compare two vehicles in the Unity scene Assets/Prototype/RaceFeel/Scenes/AssetLearningLab.unity: the Porsche Carrera from the RaceFeel prototype and the imported ASR Ferrari 643. This version includes observations made after the Ferrari was actually exported from Blender, imported into Unity, textured, and inspected in the scene. The original lesson remains available as the pre-import baseline.

Verification status

The Ferrari used for this comparison is Reference_Ferrari643_Complete, instantiated from Assets/Prototype/RaceFeel/LearningAssets/ASR/Ferrari643/Ferrari643_Learning_Complete.fbx.

  • Geometry: verified in Unity; the imported root has 188 renderers and bounds of approximately 2.21 × 1.00 × 4.51 m.
  • Textures: 80 source .dds/.png files were copied into the learning asset folder; Unity recognized 74 as Texture2D assets.
  • Materials: Unity generated 33 external materials under the Ferrari learning folder.
  • Shader: imported materials use Universal Render Pipeline/Lit; this is an observed import state, not the final Overdrive material standard.
  • Damage: damage.dds was removed from the learning copy's asr-car-body.mat normal-map slot because the game has no damage presentation.
  • Known source problems: the visible tire-sidewall lettering is incorrectly oriented, and the Marlboro / A. Prost decal is skewed on the side body. These remain documented defects for analysis; they are not silently presented as production-ready.

1. Mesh and geometry

The mesh is the polygonal surface that gives an object its three-dimensional shape. Geometry is broader than polygon count. It includes:

  • volumes and curves;
  • topology: how polygons connect;
  • modeled details;
  • separate parts and hierarchy;
  • collision geometry;
  • LOD variants.

The Porsche prefab contains 44 objects, including body, doors, glass, wheels, brakes, mirrors, lamps, spoiler, steering wheel, collider, and a disabled CARRERA_LOW visual variant. That modularity is useful: different parts can receive different materials and can be animated or replaced independently.

The imported Ferrari preserves a much deeper ASR hierarchy. It contains dedicated body sections, cockpit parts, wings, suspension, wheel assemblies, tire meshes, dashboard pieces, camera markers, and the driver-related objects. The hierarchy is useful for studying an authentic race-car asset, but it is more complex than a clean production-ready Overdrive prefab.

2. Silhouette

The silhouette is the outer contour that remains if the object is filled completely black. It is one of the fastest ways to identify whether an asset belongs to the correct category.

AssetSilhouette communicatesPossible Overdrive role
Porsche CarreraClosed-roof civilian sports carCharacter car, paddock vehicle, cutscene prop
ASR Ferrari 6431991 open-wheel Formula carRace car, grid, garage, replay, hero shot

Texture cannot turn the Porsche into an F1 car. It can make the Porsche a better Overdrive civilian car. Conversely, the Ferrari silhouette already belongs to the racing category, even if its surface, material count, and geometry detail need production treatment.

3. Proportions

Proportion means the relationship between parts: wheel diameter to body height, cockpit size to car length, wing width to chassis width, and so on.

Anime styling does not require every proportion to become unrealistic. Overdrive should preserve important historical anchors in its F1 cars: low nose, open cockpit, exposed wheels, and period-appropriate wings. Stylization can come from simplification, clearer shapes, controlled exaggeration, and graphic surface treatment.

For the Porsche, real-world road-car proportions are acceptable if its role is a civilian vehicle. The Ferrari preserves the correct 1991 F1 proportions and silhouette anchors. That does not automatically make either asset final: proportions answer “does this belong to the category?”, not “does this already belong to the final visual identity?”

4. UVs

UVs are the 2D layout that tells a texture where to appear on a 3D mesh. Imagine cutting the surface of a cardboard object and unfolding it flat. The unfolded pattern is the UV layout.

Good geometry can still be difficult to repaint if:

  • UV islands are too small or fragmented;
  • left and right sides overlap when unique markings are needed;
  • the texture resolution is wasted on hidden surfaces;
  • decals stretch across seams;
  • text appears mirrored, rotated, or skewed on one side.

The Ferrari gives us concrete examples. Its tire meshes use separate left/right sidewall materials: 9X_GWAL-L uses G9X_twall-l.dds, while 9X_GWAL-R uses G9X_twall-r.dds. The visible GOODYEAR/EAGLE lettering is incorrectly oriented. The Marlboro / A. Prost marking is on the separate fer_body-6 decal mesh using 643_BODY_DECAL and 643_INT_Decals_DIF.dds. Those observations show why a readable logo is a UV and decal-placement test, not only a color test.

The ASR import also creates a material-routing problem. The learning import produced 33 external materials and 74 recognized texture assets. A production car should consolidate these into semantic groups such as paint, carbon, interior, glass, rubber, brakes, and LEDs rather than carrying every source material forward unchanged.

5. Texture and surface

A texture is an image applied to the UV layout. It can provide base color, painted shadows, decals, roughness information, normal information, or emission. Overdrive's art bible prioritizes painted textures and explicitly prohibits normal maps for the car body workflow.

The Porsche currently uses CARRERA_4096.png as its main base texture. Its original surface communicates a more realistic sports car through paint, glass, tire detail, and material separation. A restyle could preserve the mesh while changing the surface language:

  • replace realistic decals and logos;
  • reduce micro-detail;
  • paint clearer color blocks;
  • remove or reduce normal maps;
  • make highlights intentional instead of physically descriptive.

The Ferrari demonstrates a different surface issue. Its original source textures contain historical livery, tire lettering, cockpit details, and damage-related data. The learning copy keeps the useful livery and detail textures but removes damage.dds from the body material. This is a surface correction that does not require changing the body mesh.

6. Material and shader

A material combines textures and numeric responses such as smoothness, metallic value, specular color, emission, and transparency. A shader is the program that uses those inputs to render the surface.

Unity inspection found that the Porsche's main material, Assets/Prototype/RaceFeel/Materials/CARRERA_4096.mat, uses Universal Render Pipeline/Lit, smoothness 0.483, metallic 0.102, environment reflections, and shadow reception. Its matte material uses the same shader with smoothness and metallic set to zero.

The Ferrari learning materials also imported as Universal Render Pipeline/Lit. That is sufficient for this anatomy lesson, but it is not the final Overdrive material mapping. The art bible calls for controlled Simple Lit materials for car paint, carbon, cockpit, and tires, with the visual identity carried primarily by painted textures.

The Ferrari body provides a concrete material diagnosis: asr-car-body.mat originally had damage.dds connected to _BumpMap with _NORMALMAP enabled. Clearing that slot removed the dark damage artifacts while preserving the main body texture. This is the difference between removing a bad material input and discarding useful geometry.

7. Prefab and Unity role

A prefab is the reusable Unity package that combines an object's hierarchy, components, materials, colliders, and configuration. The same mesh can be useful or useless depending on how it is packaged.

8. Context and camera

Context is the asset's role, placement, neighbors, lighting, and camera. A Porsche viewed in a warm garage beside an Overdrive mechanic can feel correct as a character vehicle. The same Porsche placed on the starting grid beside 16 Formula cars would communicate the wrong gameplay role.

The Ferrari was inspected beside the Porsche in a neutral learning scene. That context makes the comparison useful, but it is not a final game presentation. The white/gray Porsche and textured Ferrari are being compared as assets, not judged as a finished race scene.

Decision vocabulary

After this comparison, use one of four decisions:

  • Use directly: geometry, surface, and role already fit.
  • Re-style: geometry and role fit; surface/materials do not.
  • Simplify or remodel: category is correct, but geometry detail or proportions fight the style.
  • Reference only: valuable for history or study, but not a good production base.

Exercise

Classify each observation by the layer where the primary problem lives:

  1. The Porsche has a realistic paint response but a usable civilian silhouette.
  2. The Ferrari's GOODYEAR/EAGLE lettering is reversed on a visible tire sidewall.
  3. The Ferrari's Marlboro / A. Prost decal is skewed on the side body.
  4. The Ferrari body shows dark damage artifacts because damage.dds was connected as a normal map.
  5. The Ferrari imports 154 object/action clips when the lesson only needs a clean static asset comparison.
Expected classification

1 is primarily a material/shader issue. 2 is a UV/texture/material assignment issue. 3 is a UV/decal placement issue. 4 is a material input issue. 5 is an animation import/pipeline issue, not evidence that the mesh itself is unusable.
