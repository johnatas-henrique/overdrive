Overdrive Asset Production

Learning track

A navigable map of the asset-production lessons

How to use this index: complete the available lessons in order. The roadmap below is planning information, not a claim that future lessons already exist. Each lesson separates visual judgment, measurable evidence, and production decisions.

Available lessons

  1. Lesson 1 — Style, Technique, Standard, and Conformance Checking

    Purpose: distinguish visual identity from the technical method used to render it and from measurable production standards.

    Outcome: evaluate a generated image with an eight-point checklist instead of relying only on “I like it”.

  2. Lesson 2 — Programmatic Palette Checks

    Purpose: learn what a script can measure in an image: dimensions, background color, color distance, and dominant colors.

    Outcome: understand what the machine proved and what still requires visual inspection.

  3. Lesson 3 — Anatomy of a 3D Asset

    Purpose: locate problems in the mesh, silhouette, proportions, UVs, textures, materials, shader, prefab, or context.

    Case study: compare the Porsche Carrera and the Unity-imported ASR Ferrari 643 in AssetLearningLab.unity.

    Outcome: diagnose the Ferrari's damage-map artifact, material fragmentation, inverted tire lettering, and skewed Marlboro / A. Prost decal without confusing those problems with geometry.

  4. Lesson 4 — Materials, Shaders, and Surface Response

    Purpose: understand how textures, material properties, shaders, and lighting combine to produce the visible surface.

    Case study: compare the Porsche's reflective URP/Lit material with the Ferrari's imported materials and removed damage-map input.

    Outcome: identify surface problems before changing geometry and perform controlled one-variable material experiments.

  5. Lesson 5 — UVs, Decals, and Mirrored Text

    Purpose: understand how images are placed on meshes and how mirrored, rotated, stretched, or skewed decals are diagnosed.

    Case study: inspect the Ferrari's tire lettering and Marlboro / A. Prost body decal.

    Outcome: isolate texture, UV, material-assignment, and mesh-orientation problems; apply a targeted material-space correction to the tire lettering; and classify the crooked body decals as texture-space defects without remodeling the car.

  6. Lesson 6 — Blender Asset Preparation and Export Validation

    Purpose: verify effective visibility, collections, variants, parentage, animation dependencies, and visual completeness before exporting an FBX.

    Case study: reproduce the Ferrari export failures involving hidden body objects, the CINTURE_ON/OFF variants, separate car/driver exports, and duplicated Unity animation clips.

    Outcome: export the smallest intentional scope and prove that the Unity import matches the Blender source.

  7. Lesson 7 — Unity Import, Material Routing, and Prefab Assembly

    Purpose: trace an FBX through Unity's importer, subassets, renderer material slots, textures, shaders, scene instances, and prefab decisions.

    Case study: explain why two Ferrari instances accidentally shared materials and how to isolate a second-car shader experiment.

    Outcome: distinguish embedded materials from editable external materials and decide whether an imported model is still a learning reference or ready for production prefab assembly.

  8. Lesson 8 — Practical Restyling of a Simple Prop

    Purpose: change surface treatment without changing geometry, hierarchy, or presentation.

    Case study: restyle prop_wall_A using isolated copies of GARAGES.mat and FENCE.mat.

    Outcome: perform a one-variable material experiment and prove that the original prop remains unchanged.

  9. Lesson 9 — Vehicle Restyling: From Art Bible to Surface Specification

    Purpose: turn the art bible into a concrete vehicle surface contract before remodeling the vehicle.

    Case study: map vehicle surface domains and compare Simple Lit, Unlit, cel/ramp, and depth/normal outline candidates.

    Outcome: classify shader and material candidates from fixed-camera evidence and record a reproducible production surface specification.

  10. Lesson 10 — Animation, Cutscene Assets, and Presentation Separation

    Purpose: separate car animation, driver animation, validation actions, and cinematic presentation.

    Case study: classify VAL_* actions, preserve the car/driver boundary, and avoid treating all imported clips as production content.

    Outcome: assign motion ownership, fit the driver in the correct order, and export only intentional animation content.

  11. Lesson 11 — 2D Generation Smoke Test and Model Selection

    Purpose: prove a reproducible Krea2 + LoRA 2D baseline before committing any design to multi-view or 3D reconstruction.

    Provided procedure: assemble the fixed Krea2 node graph manually from the lesson's connection contract; do not change model, prompt, or parameters during the first run.

    Case study: reproduce the user's successful Krea2 setup, test the LoRA and enhancer one variable at a time, then generate one original tier-one vehicle concept with the Fujishima + Matsuri style anchor.

    Outcome: prove technical reproducibility, classify structural and style failures, record provenance, and approve only candidates with reconstruction potential.

  12. Lesson 12 — Technical Candidate Evaluation: 11-Point Asset Audit READY

    Purpose: evaluate technically acceptable F1 models before choosing a first Overdrive pipeline candidate.

    Outcome: inspect format route, hierarchy, transforms, pivots, mesh cost, UVs, materials, animation, LODs, colliders, cockpit integration, and license with reproducible Blender/Unity checks.

  13. March CG891 — Live Technical Evaluation Worksheet ACTIVE

    Purpose: record measured evidence from the first real application of Lesson 12 instead of relying on a generic checklist.

    Current findings: 28,052 triangles, 90 mesh objects, 25 empties, transparent body caused by DDS alpha interpretation, object scale 0.01, useful local wheel/suspension parents, and no global car-root object.
