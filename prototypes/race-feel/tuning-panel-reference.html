RaceFeel Tuning Panel — Field Reference

Prototype documentation · 2026-08-02 · Source: Assets/Prototype/RaceFeel/RaceFeelTuningPanel.cs, Assets/Prototype/RaceFeel/ArcadeCar.cs, design/gdd/car-definition-data.md

This document explains every field of the runtime tuning panel (press Tab in Play Mode), the physics model each one controls, and the values loaded by the four car presets: Car 20, Car 12, Car 4 (converted from the GDD stat system) and Car proto (the feel-calibrated reference car).

1. Steering model — how it works

The prototype uses the 1-state steering capacity curve (playtest 2026-08-02). Every physics tick the maximum allowed yaw rate is the minimum of three independent limits:

maxYaw = min(steeringCeiling(v), v / minTurnRadius, gripCeiling(v))
  • Steering ceiling — falls linearly (or with a shape exponent) from maxSteerLow at low speed to maxSteerHigh at top speed. This is the design cap: more turn authority at low speed, less at high speed.
  • Min turn circle — v / minTurnRadius. A real car has a minimum turning circle; this prevents pivoting in place at standstill.
  • Grip ceiling — effectiveGrip × maxLateralG × 9.81 / v. The car cannot turn tighter than its lateral grip allows; above it the car understeers smoothly.

The executed yaw is steerEma × maxYaw applied directly to Rigidbody.angularVelocity.y (no torque inertia — instant arcade response).

10. FAQ — playtest questions (2026-08-02)

Q1. How does grip map to rad/s? Is the lift-off bonus being throttled by grip?

Grip enters as gripCeiling = g × 9.81 / v rad/s — it is not a fixed rate, it falls with 1/speed:

Speed1g4.49g (Car 20)20g (proto)
100 km/h0.351.597.06
200 km/h0.180.793.53
324 km/h0.110.512.18

Confirmed bug (user report): the lift-off bonus is added to the steering ceiling before the min() with gripCeiling. For low-grip cars the grip ceiling binds first — Car 20 at 200 km/h: steering 2.10 + 0.6 = 2.70, but min(2.70, 0.79) = 0.79. Lifting off never helps the cars that need it. Physically, lifting off transfers weight forward = more front grip. Proposed fix: apply the bonus as a multiplier of maxLateralAccel (e.g. ×1.25) so low-grip cars gain cornering proportional to their capacity.

Q2. Why 3.5 m/s² for coast decel?

Empirical, calibrated in the 2026-08-02 playtest. Rigidbody damping alone is asymptotic — the car never stops (~3 km/h forever). 3.5 m/s² ≈ 12.6 km/h lost per second with no pedals: perceptible engine-braking without being abrupt. 300→0 km/h without brakes ≈ 24 s. No external source — it is a feel knob.

Q3. Why does Car 20 cap at 305 km/h with max 310 — even raising engine power?

Because enginePower is only the launch ceiling. Actual acceleration is min(enginePower, powerPerMass/v). Above 262 km/h the P/m term (1012/v) is smaller and dominates. The linear damping then defines the physical terminal:

vmax_real = sqrt(P/m ÷ damping) = sqrt(1012 / 0.14) = 85 m/s = 306 km/h

Raising enginePower changes nothing — min() picks the P/m term. To reach 310: lower damping (0.13 → 317) or raise P/m. The damping formula (enginePower/(1.15×vmax)) was designed for the old constant-force model; with the power curve it no longer lands on the declared vmax.

Q4. Why is the P/m power curve imperceptible on the GDD cars?

No preset sets P/m — all four cars inherit 1012. And for the weak cars the curve never binds before the speed cap:

Car 20 (13.89)1012/13.89 = 72.9 m/s (262 km/h)Only the last ~48 km/h.

Q5. Does controlThreshold (Stability) cut yaw? Car 4 is uncornerable — correct?

Confirmed bug — FIXED 2026-08-02. controlThreshold used to multiply the grip stack (clamp(base×surface×tire×control, 0.2, 1.2)), multiplying maxLateralAccel. Car 4: 0.2 × 2.08g = 0.416g effective — at 100 km/h the minimum radius was 27.8²/(0.416×9.81) = 189 m, on a 14 m track with 70 m corners. The GDD defines stability as "harder to lose control" — resistance to sliding, NOT cornering ability (that is the Grip stat). Fix (applied): cornering capacity now comes from the Grip stat only. Stability acts on the slip behavior: slide threshold scales with controlThreshold (0.25 → 0.05 slip for stability 0.2), recovery rate scales gripAlignRate × controlThreshold, and slide speed loss becomes driftSpeedLoss × (2 − controlThreshold). A low-stability car now corners as well as its grip allows but slides early, recovers slowly, and loses more speed while sliding.

Q6. Is linear damping fixed or per-car? Why does it exist?

Per-car: the proto uses 0.28 fixed, GDD presets derive enginePower/(1.15×vmax) → 0.140 / 0.095 / 0.075. The old "Fixed constants" heading meant "not editable in the panel", not "equal across cars" — renamed in §4. It exists as linear drag F = −c·v: without it the car would hit the speed cap with full force (a step); with it the acceleration dies near the top — the "sag" in the last km/h. The 1.15 factor puts the natural terminal 15% above the declared cap so the cap cuts exactly at vmax — but with the power curve the real terminal is sqrt(P/m ÷ damping), so the formula needs revisiting (e.g. damping = P/m ÷ (1.15×vmax)²).
